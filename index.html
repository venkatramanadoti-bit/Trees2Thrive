INTRODUCTION

What is Agroforestry?

Agroforestry is an integrated land-use approach in which trees are combined with agricultural crops, livestock or other farm activities. It brings tree-based components into farming systems and can support production, livelihoods and environmental sustainability.

04 — FARMING SYSTEMS

What Agroforestry Practices Are Promoted by Government?

Agroforestry can involve different combinations of trees, crops, horticulture, pasture and livestock depending on local conditions.

🌳🌾

Agri-Silviculture

Agricultural crops are combined with trees or forestry components.

🌳🐄

Agri-Silvipastoral

Agriculture, trees and pasture or livestock components are combined.

🌳🍎🌾

Agri-Horti-Silviculture

Agricultural crops are combined with horticultural and tree components.

🍎🐄

Horti-Pastoral

Horticultural trees are combined with pasture-based systems.

🌳➖

Boundary Plantation

Trees are planted along farm boundaries, field edges or bunds.

🌱🌳

Tree-Crop Intercropping

Agricultural crops are grown alongside trees as part of an integrated farming system.

06 — ENVIRONMENT

What Are the Environmental Benefits of Agroforestry?

Agroforestry can provide ecosystem services such as soil conservation, biodiversity support, carbon storage and microclimate benefits.

🌍

Carbon Storage

Trees store carbon in their biomass and can contribute to climate mitigation.

💧

Water Conservation

Tree-based systems can contribute to soil and water conservation.

🪱

Soil Health

Agroforestry can support soil conservation and contribute to soil organic matter.

🦋

Biodiversity

Trees in agricultural landscapes can provide habitat for different organisms.

🌡️

Microclimate

Trees can provide shade and influence local farm microclimate.

🌧️

Climate Resilience

Integrated tree-based farming systems can help farms respond to climate variability.
